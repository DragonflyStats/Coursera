Edward O’Brien is a waifish young aesthete and a classicist at heart, whose detached, high-minded demeanor is a direct product of his unique upbringing. 
The son of a pragmatic Anglican Rector, O’Brien spent his formative years in an "ecclesiastical void" that doubled as a multi-denominational logging camp. 
Under his father's strict mantra that "the devil makes work for idle hands," O’Brien watched a diverse swarm of local children—many of them Roman Catholic, sent by parents just to 
get them out of the house—build a fierce esprit de corps while fetching firewood and chopping timber in the manse woodshed.

This environment left O'Brien with a deep-seated ambivalence toward domestic structures and a legendary lack of impulse control. 
Rather than learning the names of the children around him, he became a detached observer of human nature, a trait that makes him a highly unpredictable travel 
companion for his friend Kevin Comerford. 

Driven by whim rather than any specific ideological direction, O'Brien treats life as a grand, unscripted farce. 
He is a bon vivant and a hypnotic raconteur who is entirely at home striking up strange, random conversations with strangers in taverns, navigating the world under what 
Comerford calls a "Targeted Perception Deficiency" that ignores grand monuments but zeroes in on a glass of Scotch or a pickled onion.
